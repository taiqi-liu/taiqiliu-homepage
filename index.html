01 — About

A device physicist at heart, an IC researcher by training.

Short version: I make tiny capacitors remember — and then I try to make them remember reliably, for a very long time, at very low voltage.

My work sits at the intersection of materials, devices, and circuits. On the materials side, I study hafnia-based ferroelectrics — especially Zr-doped HfO2 (HZO) in both its ferroelectric and antiferroelectric phases. On the device side, I fabricate and measure MFM capacitors, ferroelectric tunnel junctions (FTJs), and ferroelectric FETs (FeFETs). On the systems side, I care about what those devices actually enable: dense non-volatile memory and computing-in-memory for edge AI.

I’m currently finishing my master’s (expected 2028) and preparing applications for Fall 2028 PhD programs in integrated circuits and electronic devices. I’m broadly interested in any IC direction where device physics still matters — emerging memory, heterogeneous integration, and hardware for AI — not just ferroelectrics.

What I’m looking for in a PhD lab

A group where I can go from film growth to array-level demonstration — and where reliability is treated as a first-class research question, not an afterthought.

  • Fabrication access + serious electrical characterization
  • Device–circuit co-design, not devices in isolation
  • Open, collaborative culture with strong mentorship
02 — Research

Research focus

Tap any line to expand it. These are the four threads that run through almost everything I do.

R.01

HZO ferroelectrics — wake-up, fatigue & the orthorhombic phase

Why does HZO need to “wake up,” why does it get tired, and how do we stabilize the ferroelectric phase without either?

I study ALD-grown HZO thickness and Zr-ratio series (7–12 nm), annealing conditions, and electrode effects. Using PUND, P–V hysteresis, and endurance cycling, I map how remanent polarization, coercive field, and leakage evolve — and test La/Y doping and interface layers as routes to wake-up-free operation.

R.02

FeFETs for embedded non-volatile memory

Memory windows are easy to report and hard to keep. I work on keeping them.

My FeFET work focuses on the memory window vs. reliability trade-off: charge trapping at the ferroelectric/interlayer interface, retention at temperature, and disturb in small arrays. Recent devices show a ~1.8 V window with 10-year extrapolated retention at 85 °C in simulation-backed measurements — the next step is statistical data across a full wafer.

R.03

Antiferroelectric HZO & FTJs for analog computing

Double hysteresis loops look like a problem — until you need an analog, energy-efficient synapse.

Antiferroelectric HZO offers near-zero remanence, fast switching, and high endurance — attractive for capacitors in DRAM-like and energy-storage roles. In FTJs, I explore multi-level conductance states for vector–matrix multiplication, measuring linearity, symmetry, and cycle-to-cycle variation for neuromorphic inference.

R.04

Reliability physics — from defects to lifetime models

Breakdown, imprint, and fatigue are not footnotes. They decide whether a device ever leaves the lab.

I’m building a small reliability workflow: TDDB and bipolar stress testing, temperature-accelerated retention, and simple physics-based lifetime models that connect defect generation in HZO to circuit-level failure rates. This thread will take me to Lingshui in January 2027 for the REACT workshop on advanced IC reliability, including its ferroelectric memory reliability panel.
